# Game Concepts

Concepts for a hybrid-casual iOS puzzle game (2D, SpriteKit, no forced ads, sold through power-ups and fail-screen offers). **Patchwork Valley** is in development. The rest are a backlog for future games.

Design rules that apply to every concept:
- Calm and turn-based, with no timers.
- Levels are generated by the game, never hand-built.
- The core mechanic regularly produces a near-win ("so close") moment. That moment is what makes power-ups sell.

## 1. Patchwork Valley (in development)

**Mashup:** a Dorfromantik-style landscape builder plus a Woodoku-style piece tray.

Players place hex tiles (forest, field, river, village) to grow a landscape. Matching edges score points. Completing a quest, such as "grow this forest to 12 tiles", earns more tiles. The run ends when the tiles run out.

- **Near-win:** tiles run out while a quest is one tile from complete.
- **Power-ups:** +5 tiles, rotate or undo, swap the next tile, see the next three tiles.
- **Meta:** every run leaves a finished little world. Saving it to a gallery makes the collection free to produce.
- **Risk:** Dorfromantik (Toukana, a paid game on PC, Switch, PlayStation, Xbox and Mac) has announced an iOS port. The placement mechanic is shared genre territory. Patchwork Valley needs its own look and theme; a fabric-patch, quilt-stitch style fits the name.

**Ideas on file (not planned yet):**
- **An antagonist.** Three shapes, lightest first. A clever deal, like 2048's awkward spawns: cheap and invisible, but it can feel unfair. Creeping brambles that take one empty spot every few turns until fenced in: one new rule, and it fits the cozy mood. A rival builder that places a tile after each of the player's, growing from the far side of the crater until the two collide: the Carcassonne and StarCraft feel, but a big shift from calm that needs AI design and a board that still reads clearly. The game already asks a lot (the match rule, rivers, fog, mountains, lakes, the crater edge, quests, the tile limit, running out of room), so an antagonist should replace pressure rather than add to it. A rival fits best as its own mode or a later-valley twist (valley 10 on), proved first with the bot and a debug switch.

## 2. Prism

**Mashup:** laser-and-mirror routing, a piece tray and color mixing.

Players place mirrors and prisms to route colored light beams to targets. Colors mix (red + blue = purple), so each route has to be planned.

- **Near-win:** every target is lit except one.
- **Power-ups:** an extra mirror, a beam splitter, rotating a locked piece.
- **Why it fits:** glowing light looks great in SpriteKit (additive blending, effect nodes), and no game dominates the niche.

## 3. Potion Shop

**Mashup:** water sort plus real color mixing.

Players pour liquids between flasks to fill customer orders. Pouring yellow into blue makes green, so this is sorting with a plan behind it.

- **Near-win:** one flask is off from the order.
- **Power-ups:** an extra flask (the "extra parking spot" of this genre), undo, a color filter.
- **Why it fits:** water sort is a proven, massive genre, and color mixing makes it fresh.

## 4. Critter Chain

**Mashup:** Triple Town-style placement plus a food chain.

Players place animals on a grid, and each animal follows a rule: foxes chase rabbits and rabbits eat carrots. The goal is to set up chain reactions that clear the board before it fills.

- **Near-win:** one open square is left and a chain is one critter short.
- **Power-ups:** swap, remove, preview the next critter.
- **Why it fits:** charm and personality, the thing that drove Meowdoku's 2026 breakout.

## 5. Canal Keeper

**Mashup:** a turn-based Pipe Mania plus a piece tray.

Water advances one step per turn. Players lay canal pieces to irrigate fields before the village floods.

- **Near-win:** the water is visibly one tile from a field or a house.
- **Power-ups:** a sandbag wall, "hold the water one turn", an extra piece.
- **Why it fits:** it has the most built-in tension without any timer. The trade-off is that its pacing is the closest to an action puzzle.
